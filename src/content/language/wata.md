<span class="kt">wata</span> is the kahu-tok word for water. it is one of the foundational roots of the bixel 1000.

the word's english inheritance is direct. bunker-tongue use of "water" simplified across the dark century into a form that bixel preserved in kahu-tok. the present-day pronunciation has shifted slightly from the english original but the continuity is unbroken.

<span class="kt">wata</span> appears in central geographic compounds inside the caldera. <span class="proper">hart-wata</span> (heart-water) names the great lake at the center of the caldera floor. <span class="kt">komi-wata</span> is the river that runs through sun-tera, feeding the lower fields and joining hart-wata at the basin center; <span class="kt">komi</span> appears to be a productive root with broader applications in kahu-tok beyond this compound.

whether <span class="kt">wata</span> distinguishes between fresh and contaminated water at the lexical level is open canon. the kahu have practical reason to make the distinction; the great western sea is toxic and unusable water at scale, while hart-wata is the freshwater that sustains caldera life. the language presumably has ways of marking this difference, but whether through separate roots, modifiers, or only context is not yet documented in the archive.
