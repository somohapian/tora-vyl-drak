<span class="kt">micasu</span> is the kahu-tok word for welcome. it is one of the organic accretions to the language, words that entered the lexicon from the bunker populations the <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/zou-esh">zou-esh</a> brought home rather than from <a class="wiki-link" href="/people/bixel">bixel</a>'s engineered core. its source is the spanish phrase "mi casa es su casa," "my house is your house," compressed across generations of use into a single word.

the compression happened in the centuries after <a class="wiki-link" href="/events/the-first-descent">the first descent</a>, as spanish-speaking survivors integrated into <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a> life and their habitual phrase of welcome was overheard, picked up, and shortened by speakers without a strong claim on the original grammar. by approximately 2900 ad it had stabilized in its current form. by the present day it is a native-feeling kahu-tok word that few kahu connect to its romance-language origin without prompting.

the related kahu-tok root <span class="kt">kasa</span> means house. the engineered word <span class="kt">ven-kasa</span>, "come-house," is the formal kahu-tok term for the newcomer settlement at <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/nu-tera">nu-tera</a> and overlaps in function with micasu in some contexts. but micasu carries the particular warmth of the spanish original. the kahu use it as a greeting, especially across thresholds and at first arrivals. it appears at the close of the wiki's homepage at the place a kahu would say it to a stranger who has just walked in.
