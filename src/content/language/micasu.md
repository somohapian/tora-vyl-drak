<span class="kt">micasu</span> is the kahu-tok word for welcome. it is one of the organic accretions to the language, words that entered the lexicon by use rather than from <a class="wiki-link" href="/people/bixel">bixel</a>'s engineered core.

micasu began as five words of spanish. mi casa es su casa, my house is your house, the southern caravan's greeting across the founding fences. the phrase was said whole for generations, then said fast, then said as one word. by the middle <a class="wiki-link" href="/eras/age-of-tapestry">age of tapestry</a> the record shows micasu as a single word meaning welcome, and by the bloom it had drifted to the full sense it carries now: what is mine is already yours. the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a> did not shorten the hospitality. they shortened the distance to it.

the related kahu-tok root <span class="kt">kasa</span> means house. the engineered word <span class="kt">ven-kasa</span>, "come-house," is the formal kahu-tok term for the newcomer settlement at <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/nu-tera">nu-tera</a> and overlaps in function with micasu in some contexts. the kahu use micasu as a greeting, especially across thresholds and at first arrivals. it appears at the close of the wiki's homepage.
