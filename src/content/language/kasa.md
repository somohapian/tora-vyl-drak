<span class="kt">kasa</span> is the kahu-tok word for house, home, or dwelling. it is one of the foundational roots of the bixel 1000.

the apparent romance-language inheritance (spanish "casa," portuguese "casa") is conspicuous. whether bixel deliberately adopted the form because it was already in active use among spanish-speaking bunker survivors during the dark century, or coined it independently as a transparent root, is open canon. <span class="kt">micasu</span>, the kahu-tok word for welcome, derives from the spanish phrase "mi casa es su casa" and shares its root with <span class="kt">kasa</span>.

the word appears in major kahu compounds. <span class="proper">hot-kasa</span> (warm-house, the warmth) names the principal kahu settlement, the dense three-dimensional warren of carved chambers in the hearth that houses approximately one hundred and twenty thousand people. <span class="kt">ven-kasa</span> (come-house) is the formal kahu-tok term for the newcomer settlement at nu-tera, the quarantine zone and integration belt where arrivals from below the spore line begin their kahu life.

the semantic range of <span class="kt">kasa</span> covers from the small private chamber to the institutional building. context disambiguates. the kahu's three-dimensional communal architecture in hot-kasa means that "kasa" can refer to a sleeping chamber, a family suite, a sub-community working space, or the entire settlement, depending on what the speaker means.
