<span class="kt">shen</span> is the kahu-tok word for life. it is one of the foundational roots of the bixel 1000 and one of the productive compounding roots of the language.

the word appears in compounds across multiple semantic domains. <span class="kt">peli-shen</span> (danger-life), the radiotrophic fungus that has rendered the lowlands lethal. <span class="kt">ston-shen</span> (rock-life), the chemosynthetic lichen-bacteria paste that sustained the bunker survivors. <span class="proper">ol-tri shen</span> (old-tree life), the forest preserve in the wilds. each of these compounds names a form of life by qualifying <span class="kt">shen</span> with the relevant modifier in importance-first order.

the semantic range of <span class="kt">shen</span> covers biological life broadly. plant life, animal life, fungal life, the more abstract sense of "the conditions of life." whether it extends to non-biological senses (the kahu's biotech systems, warden, the agent stack, all of which are "alive" in some readings) is open canon. the language allows the extension; whether the kahu's actual usage takes it is a matter of practice rather than rule.

whether <span class="kt">shen</span> appears as a freestanding word in modern kahu-tok or only as a compounding element is open canon. the bunker-tongue inheritance and bixel's engineered design both suggest the word may have a freestanding usage, but the dominant pattern in the archive is its appearance in compounds.
