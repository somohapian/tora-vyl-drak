<span class="kt">peli</span> is the kahu-tok word for danger. it is one of the foundational roots of the bixel 1000. it functions both as a noun (a danger, the concept of danger) and as an interjection used in shortened form as a warning exclamation.

the word's most prominent compound is <span class="kt">peli-shen</span> (danger-life), the radiotrophic fungus that has rendered the lowlands lethal. the importance-first construction places the danger first; that the danger is alive is named second. the word ordering reflects the kahu's relationship to the organism: the danger is the more important feature, and the language refuses to soften that by burying it.

in zou-esh field operations, the shortened form <span class="kt">!peli</span> functions as a sharp warning call. a zou-esh alerting a partner to immediate danger uses the exclamation either alone or as the leading element of a more specific warning. the exclamation is one of the few cases in kahu-tok where a single-word utterance carries a complete operational meaning. the protocols around its use, the situations that warrant it, the response a partner is expected to perform when they hear it, are open canon in formal codification but are part of zou-esh training.

the full inventory of <span class="kt">peli</span> compounds beyond <span class="kt">peli-shen</span> is open canon. plausibly the root appears in other warning constructions, in the formal vocabulary of medical practice (where degrees of biological danger require precise language), and in the kahu's environmental management terminology. specific instances will be added as they are confirmed.
