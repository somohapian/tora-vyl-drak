<span class="kt">tok-peles</span> is the kahu-tok term for the simplified english-creole patois spoken by the bunker survivors during the dark century. the english rendering is the bastard tongue. the literal reading of the kahu-tok compound is place-talk, indicating the language's character as a localized survival speech rather than an inherited formal tongue.

the word is itself kahu-tok, not tok-peles. it was coined during the early age of tapestry by the founding kahu community as they were transitioning from the bunker speech to the engineered language. the name carries a mild self-deprecating note. the kahu are not embarrassed by tok-peles, the speech of their ancestors, but they did choose to leave it behind.

the structure of the compound is open canon at a fine-grained level. <span class="kt">tok</span>, the root for talk or speech, appears in importance-first position. <span class="kt">peles</span>, an apparent locative root, follows. this is the same root order as <span class="kt">kahu-tok</span> (kahu-talk), and the parallel construction is likely deliberate. both terms name a language by its community of practice; kahu-tok names the speech of the kahu community, tok-peles names the speech of the place where survival was happening.

the concept entry for the bastard tongue covers the actual language behavior (grammar, vocabulary, history) of tok-peles as a spoken language. this entry is the dictionary entry for the kahu-tok word that names it.
