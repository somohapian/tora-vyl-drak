<span class="kt"><a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/zou-esh">zou-esh</a></span> is the kahu-tok word for the scholar-warrior order of the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a>. it is used both for the order collectively and, in the singular, for an individual member. "a zou-esh," "the zou-esh," and "the order" are all valid in english renderings; the kahu-tok form does not formally distinguish.

the root <span class="kt">zou</span> is the mil-tok's own verb, to go, a journey, reflecting the order's defining work of moving between the safety of <span class="proper"><a class="wiki-link" href="/places/tora-vyl-drak">tora vyl drak</a></span> and the dangerous world below. the root <span class="kt">esh</span> reads beyond: the <a class="wiki-link" href="/language/mil-tok">mil-tok</a> glosses the trekker as a zou-esh, a go-beyond. the compound as a whole reads, in importance-first order, with the going as the primary lexical content and the beyond as where the going goes.

the concept entry for the zou-esh as an organization covers the order's role, structure, training, and equipment in detail. this entry is the dictionary entry for the word as a word.

<span class="kt">zou-esh</span> sits inside a productive family of order vocabulary. <span class="kt">mara-skol</span> (mara-school) is the training institution at <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/the-gate">the gate</a>. <span class="kt">jefe-tri</span> is the personalized bow that each zou-esh designs. <span class="kt"><a class="wiki-link" href="/language/zou-nu">zou-nu</a></span>, the new-goer, is a first-year at mara-skol. <span class="kt"><a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kau-esh">kau-esh</a></span>, the little kahu, names those still on the path and those who left it. <span class="kt">klosh-zou</span> is the closing of the seventy-fifth mission, and the <span class="kt">zou-lao</span>, the old goers, are the half of the order that stays home.
