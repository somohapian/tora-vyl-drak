<span class="kt">hart</span> is the kahu-tok word for heart. it is one of the foundational roots of the bixel 1000.

the word carries english inheritance through the bunker speech of the dark century. its present-day kahu-tok form is essentially the english word with the same meaning, slightly modified in spelling and pronunciation but unambiguously continuous with the older root.

<span class="kt">hart</span> appears in two of the central geographic compounds of <span class="proper">tora vyl drak</span>. <span class="proper">hart-ston</span> (heart-stone) names the active volcanic cone within the caldera, the sacred place where the residual heat of the underlying volcano is most present. the construction is importance-first; the heart element leads, the stone follows. <span class="proper">hart-wata</span> (heart-water) names the great lake at the center of the caldera floor, the primary freshwater source and the geographic center of caldera life. the construction is the same.

<span class="kt">hart</span> is used in both of these compounds. the caldera has a heart-stone (the volcanic cone, source of warmth) and a heart-water (the lake, source of life). both are named with the same modifier.

whether <span class="kt">hart</span> in kahu-tok covers both the literal anatomical sense and the metaphorical sense of "the heart of something" is open canon. context suggests the metaphorical sense dominates in geographic compounds. whether the kahu use the same root for the anatomical organ in medical contexts is open canon.
