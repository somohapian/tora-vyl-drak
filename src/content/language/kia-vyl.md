<span class="kt">!kia-vyl</span> is the kahu-tok imperative construction meaning "live!" as a command. it appears as an inscription in the rock at the gate, recording the last command mara gave when she ordered her zou-esh team to continue without her while she fought the raiders. the exclamation mark in front of the word is the kahu-tok convention for marking a sharp imperative or warning utterance, the same convention used in <span class="kt">!peli</span>.

the construction breaks down into two elements. <span class="kt">kia</span> functions as an imperative marker or auxiliary that converts a verb root into a direct command. <span class="kt">vyl</span> is the verb root, here in the imperative reading. <span class="kt">vyl</span>'s usual gloss as "call" extends, in this construction, into the more general sense of being called to persist, to continue, to live. the literal meaning of the compound is something close to "be called to life" or "persist when called." the working translation is simply "live."

the inscription records the last command mara gave, in the rock at the place where she gave it. the modern zou-esh order treats the inscription as the founding utterance of their practice. the order lives at the gate, trains at the gate, and defends the gate.

the formal historical status of the inscription within the kahu archive is open canon. it functions as a quotation (mara's direct last words), as a saying that gets repeated across kahu life beyond the order, as something close to a proverb, and as canonical scripture of the zou-esh order's institutional self-understanding. how exactly these different readings are weighed against each other in different contexts is open canon.

whether <span class="kt">kia</span> appears in other imperative constructions in kahu-tok is open canon. the precise grammatical analysis of <span class="kt">kia</span> as a particle (marker, prefix, auxiliary) is open canon. what is canonical is the meaning of the compound: live.
