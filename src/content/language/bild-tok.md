the mil-tok is closed. the language is not. bixel built a thousand words, and the thousand were never the point: what she built was a way to make the rest. when a kahu needs a word, they do not ask permission and they do not wait for one to arrive. they build it, say it, and let the caldera decide. this article is the archive's account of how the building is done.

## the shape of a built word

the last part is the thing. the first part is which one.

kasa is a house. kuk-kasa is the cooking one, memo-kasa is the remembering one, sik-kasa is where you go when you are ill. the rule holds everywhere the language reaches: put what you mean at the end and walk the qualifier in front of it. ai-wata, eye-water, is tears. tok-son, talk-sound, is the voice. peli-shen, danger-life, is the thing in the lowlands that this whole world is built to outlast. a kahu hearing a built word for the first time reads it back to front and arrives at the meaning before the speaker has finished the sentence.

## the working patterns

the record's load-bearing heads and tails, the ones a maker reaches for first:

kasa makes places of purpose, and not only buildings: air-kasa is the lungs, think-kasa is the brain. the body is a house of houses. pipo makes the person who does a thing: sana-pipo heals, mira-pipo watches, buk-pipo keeps the books. ples makes the ground for a thing: slip-ples, mira-ples, koru-ples. taim makes its time: it-taim, klosh-taim. tool makes its tool: kat-tool, lok-tool, klimb-tool. sin takes away: sin-ai is blind, sin-strak is brave, fear taken out. net and no turn a word around: net-fer, no-isi, no-stret. doubling deepens or repeats: tok-tok is discussion, think-think is pondering, taim-taim is again and again. and the body maps onto the world, because the first makers had bodies before they had much else: kasa-hed is the roof, tri-arm is the branch, tri-sang is the sap, ston-maus is the cave.

## the three tests

the maker's notes in the fourth edition of the mil-tok hold three tests, and the tradition holds them as bixel's, whether or not the attribution would survive a librarian's audit.

the child test: a child hearing the word cold should be able to unpack it. if you must explain a built word, it is not built. it is broken.

the collision test: say it against the tok-kahu. if the sound already lives, walk away from it and come at the meaning from another side. the dictionary exists so that makers can check, and this is half of why the buk-kahu keep it.

the count test: two parts is a word. three parts is a word that had no choice, like hot-wata-skin, which is sweat, because nothing shorter would hold it. the record contains no fours, and the buk-kahu intend to keep it that way.

## when building fails

some meanings cannot be reached by bridge. bixel's own case is the famous one: toska, the grief-word she kept whole from russian, glossed in her own hand as the word i could not build a bridge to, so i carried it across. when the compound will not hold the meaning, a maker may carry a sound in whole, the way kaelith came in with the cats and stayed because the cats' name belonged to the people who kept them. carried words are rare on purpose. every one is a door in the language's wall, and the kahu count their doors.

## what is not built

the highest register is not for making. when the council retires a name into the language, as with mara, the shield, no maker's hand is involved. the language receives those words. it does not build them.

## how a built word becomes real

you use it. others use it. the buk-kahu mark it moving through the caldera, and when it has lived long enough to have a history, it enters the tok-kahu with its provenance written down. the dictionary does not permit words. it remembers them.

build the word. use the word. if the caldera needs it, the caldera keeps it.
