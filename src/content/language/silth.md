<span class="kt">silth</span> is the kahu-tok root for weaving, fiber, and the shaping of living textile. it is pronounced "seelth." it is a bound root, which means it appears inside compounds rather than standing alone in ordinary speech.

its most important compound is <span class="kt"><a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/the-kahu-silth">kahu-silth</a></span>, the order of bio-artisans who grow and shape the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a>'s living garments. as with every kahu vocation, the name is built from a modifier plus a core: <span class="kt">silth</span> specifies the work, <span class="kt">kahu</span> holds the community. the kahu-silth are, literally, the weaving-kahu, though the english "weaving" undersells what they do, since their cloth is alive.

the root carries the kahu's whole relationship to fiber. in a world without microchips, where biology does the work that machines once did, cloth is not an inert material. it monitors, protects, heals, and adapts. the <span class="kt">silth</span> root names that living relationship between a person and what they wear, not merely the act of making thread into fabric. the same biotech tradition, <span class="kt">vyl-tapi</span>, that produces kahu medicine and food produces the silth-worker's textiles.

whether <span class="kt">silth</span> can stand alone as a word for cloth or weaving, and the full set of compounds it forms beyond kahu-silth, are open canon.
