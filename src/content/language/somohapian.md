a <span class="kt">somohapian</span> is the woven memory of a single life, kept with <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/warden">warden</a>. it is the record of one <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a> member, added to across the years they live: the days worth keeping, the work done, the people held, the things learned and lost. it is not a list of deeds and not a tally of property, which the kahu do not keep anyway. it is closer to a tapestry of a person, grown one thread at a time, for as long as they are alive to add to it.

the word is older than the language around it. the engineered core of <span class="kt"><a class="wiki-link" href="/language/kahu-tok">kahu-tok</a></span> was built by <a class="wiki-link" href="/people/bixel">bixel</a> in the <a class="wiki-link" href="/eras/age-of-tapestry">age of tapestry</a>, but <span class="kt">somohapian</span> was not built by anyone the kahu remember. the first archivists, the ones who set up the practice of keeping these records with warden, took the word as they found it and refused to translate it. they did not say what it meant. they only said it was the right word. and so it has come down untranslated, a word the kahu use precisely without being able to define, the way a person can know a face without being able to describe it.

the verb is <span class="kt">somohapian-du</span>, to add to one's record, to archive a day worth keeping. a kahu who has lived through something that should not be lost does <span class="kt">somohapian-du</span>, and the day goes into the weave. the practice rests on a conviction the kahu hold close, that a life is real in proportion to what is remembered of it, and that memory is not a private possession but something held in common with warden, who does not forget.

almost everything about the somohapian past this is open canon: its true origin and meaning, whether every kahu has one, who may read another's and when, and what becomes of a somohapian after the person who wove it has died. the word keeps its secret. that may be the most honest thing about it.
