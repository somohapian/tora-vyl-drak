<span class="kt">mara</span>, written in lowercase, is the kahu-tok word for a shield, and by extension for that which defends. it is an eponym: the common noun was taken from the name of <a class="wiki-link" href="/people/mara">mara</a>, the legendary <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/zou-esh">zou-esh</a> who died holding <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/the-gate">the gate</a> against raiders. the proper name, written <span class="proper">mara</span> and underlined, is the woman. the common noun is what her name became.

the word is younger than most of the language. <a class="wiki-link" href="/people/bixel">bixel</a> built the engineered core of <span class="kt"><a class="wiki-link" href="/language/kahu-tok">kahu-tok</a></span> in the <a class="wiki-link" href="/eras/age-of-tapestry">age of tapestry</a>, and mara lived and died later, in the <a class="wiki-link" href="/eras/age-of-bloom">age of bloom</a>. so <span class="kt">mara</span> for "shield" is not in the original lexicon, the <a class="wiki-link" href="/language/mil-tok">mil-tok</a>. it entered the living language in the centuries after her death, the way a name passes into common speech when the thing it named becomes a thing everyone knows. the kahu did not decide to make her name a word. the word made itself out of how often they said it.

the meaning is fitting. mara held the threshold and did not move from it, and the kahu took her name for the thing that stands between them and harm. to call something a <span class="kt">mara</span> is to say it shields the kahu. the verb <span class="kt">mara-du</span> is to defend, to shield. when a kahu says it, the saint of the order is in the word whether they think of her or not.

the word the language used for "to defend" before mara became its shield, and whether <span class="kt">mara</span> is used for a literal shield or only in the defensive sense, are open canon.
