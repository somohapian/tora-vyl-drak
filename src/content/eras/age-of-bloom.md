the age of bloom is the twelve-hundred-year golden age of kahu civilization. it spans approximately 2700 to 3925 ad, from the end of the founding work of the tapestry through the long quiet stretch during which the kahu way of life reached its apex. nearly half of the kahu's recorded history is the bloom.

the bloom is defined by what stabilized. the language, kahu-tok, became fully native and produced literature, philosophy, and the ordinary speech of a settled people. the council and the warden-tenders matured into the institutions that still hold their basic shape in the present. agriculture moved past subsistence into reliable abundance. the warden modeling system, the biological supercomputer that informs kahu decision-making, was conceived, built, and brought online during the bloom. its completion is the era's defining technological achievement.

the population reached the rough stability of the present, approximately two hundred and seventy-eight thousand people in the caldera and the plateau. growth came from natural increase and from continued messenger missions across the bloom. the messenger missions evolved from the wide-ranging exploratory work of the tapestry into a more focused practice. by the middle of the bloom the missions had taken on the shape that would become the modern zou-esh order: long missions, deep watchfulness, the watch-first protocol, the lifetime mission cap. mara lived during the bloom and was the order's defining figure. the specific decade of her life is open canon, but she belongs to the bloom by tradition.

the bloom is also when the kahu's biotech tradition reached the form it now holds. the agent stack of viral and bacterial systems that maintain kahu daily life, the lifespan extension that lets kahu live into their second century, the warden-tenders' integration of the agents into a continuous ecological management practice; all of this was bloom work.

the bloom ends not in catastrophe but in slow accumulation of questions the bloom's institutions had not been built to answer. by the early 39th century, the strains were visible. resource allocation pressures had begun to require difficult tradeoffs. philosophical disagreements about lifespan and intervention had begun to take recognizable form. by 3925 ad the kahu themselves dated the end of the bloom and the beginning of the age of endura. the change was not announced. it was named in retrospect, when the difference had become clear.
