the age of bloom is the twelve-hundred-year golden age of <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a> civilization. it spans approximately 2700 to 3925 ad, from the end of the founding work of the tapestry through the long quiet stretch during which the kahu way of life reached its apex. nearly half of the kahu's recorded history is the bloom.

during the bloom, several systems stabilized. the language, kahu-tok, became fully native and produced literature, philosophy, and the ordinary speech of a settled people. <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/the-council">the council</a> and <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/the-warden-tenders">the warden-tenders</a> matured into the institutions that still hold their basic shape in the present. agriculture moved past subsistence into reliable abundance. the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/warden">warden</a> modeling system, the biological supercomputer that informs kahu decision-making, was conceived, built, and brought online during the bloom.

the population reached the rough stability of the present, approximately two hundred and seventy-eight thousand people in the caldera and the plateau. growth came from natural increase and from continued messenger missions across the bloom. the messenger missions evolved from the wide-ranging exploratory work of the tapestry into a more focused practice. by the middle of the bloom the missions had taken on the shape that would become the modern <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/zou-esh">zou-esh</a> order: long missions, deep watchfulness, the watch-first protocol, the lifetime mission cap. <a class="wiki-link" href="/people/mara">mara</a> lived during the bloom and was the order's defining figure. the specific decade of her life is open canon, but she belongs to the bloom by tradition.

the bloom's one great political rupture came at its middle, around 3250. <a class="wiki-link" href="/people/edi">edi</a>, the head of the zou-esh, an administrator wielding the order's hold on the gate and on the kahu's knowledge of the outside, gathered the powers of the deadlocked council into his own offices and took control of the caldera and the plateau, largely without bloodshed (see the <a class="wiki-link" href="/events/the-seizure">seizure</a>). it ended the kahu's first government, the eleven-hundred-year council republic of the founding, but it did not end the bloom: daily life was little disturbed and the flourishing continued. a resistance deposed and exiled him, and out of the settlement came the kahu's second government, the bicameral council that holds its shape in the present. the change was constitutional, not civilizational, which is why the golden age runs on past it to its quiet close at 3925.

the bloom is also when the kahu's biotech tradition reached the form it now holds. the agent stack of viral and bacterial systems that maintain kahu daily life, the lifespan extension that lets kahu live into their second century, the warden-tenders' integration of the agents into a continuous ecological management practice; all of this was bloom work.

the bloom ends not in catastrophe but in slow accumulation of questions the bloom's institutions had not been built to answer. by the early 39th century, the strains were visible. resource allocation pressures had begun to require difficult tradeoffs. philosophical disagreements about lifespan and intervention had begun to take recognizable form. by 3925 ad the kahu themselves dated the end of the bloom and the beginning of the <a class="wiki-link" href="/eras/age-of-endura">age of endura</a>. the change was named in retrospect rather than announced at the time.
