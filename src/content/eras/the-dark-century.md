the dark century is the era between <a class="wiki-link" href="/events/the-fracture">the fracture</a> and <a class="wiki-link" href="/events/the-first-descent">the first descent</a>. it spans approximately 2075 to 2283 ad, two hundred and eight years. it is the era before the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a>, the era during which the people who would become the kahu's ancestors survived underground, watched the surface, and waited.

the era opened with the impact day, the planetary fires of <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/the-hyper-winter">the hyper-winter</a>, and the global silence of the emp. within hours, half the world's population was dead. within years, ninety percent of the survivors had died from cold and starvation. the people who lived past that initial winnowing were the people who had been deep enough, prepared enough, lucky enough, or some combination. the kahu's ancestors, in the bunkers of the idaho rockies and the blue mountains of eastern oregon, were prepared and lucky and high enough to survive the secondary effects.

the dark century inside the bunkers was finite. the bunkers had not been designed as multi-generational arks. fuel reserves ran down. mechanical components failed without replacement parts. air-scrubber filters and water gaskets gave out and had to be cannibalized from non-essential sectors. by the mid-21st-century mark, the engineering inheritance of the bunker generation was largely gone. by the seventy-fifth year mark, the social command structures of the original entry generation had also given way. children of bunker-life and grandchildren of bunker-life were not the same people their bunker-builder ancestors had been.

food came from three sources across the long stretch. the ration, heavily guarded non-renewable pre-collapse stockpiles, reserved for the youngest and the sick. the farmed, fungiculture beds growing on recycled organic waste and small herds of hardy blind goat-like livestock bred to live underground on fungi. and the staple, <span class="kt"><a class="wiki-link" href="/objects/ston-shen">ston-shen</a></span>, the chemosynthetic lichen-bacteria paste grown on geothermal vents. ston-shen tasted strongly of sulfur. it kept humanity alive.

speech degraded across the dark century. formal english gave way to a simplified english-creole patois the speakers called tok-peles, the bastard tongue. complex tenses dropped out. vocabulary contracted. the language became operational, the language of bunker maintenance and shared work, less the language of pre-collapse civilization.

the long watch, the second half of the dark century from approximately 2133 onward, was the period during which the bunker survivors monitored the cooling of the newly formed kahu vyl caldera from the surrounding peaks. they took elevation readings, measured atmospheric composition, observed weather patterns, and waited. by 2283 ad the readings were good enough. the first descent followed. the dark century ended.

the kahu carry the dark century forward as the era that made them necessary and as the era that taught them how to live with limits. nothing in kahu culture is unmarked by the long underground. the practice of shared communal life, the philosophical commitment to <a class="wiki-link" href="/language/kahu"><span class="kt">kahu</span></a> as one word at every scale, the food economy's conservative reserves, the deep distrust of technological brittleness: all of it carries the lesson of the dark century forward.
