the age of tapestry is the founding era of the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a> civilization. it spans approximately 2120 to 2700 ad, from <a class="wiki-link" href="/events/the-first-descent">the first descent</a> into the caldera through the centuries of construction, language-building, and institutional formation that produced the kahu world. the era's name is the great coalescence, sometimes given alone, sometimes paired with tapestry. tapestry was the metaphor the kahu used for what they were building, and the great coalescence was their term for the building process.

the era opened with the first descent, around 2120 ad, and the immediate work of establishing chamber-life in <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/the-hearth">the hearth</a> and beginning agriculture on <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/sun-tera">sun-tera</a>. early generations spoke the bastard tongue, but the impulse toward an engineered replacement language emerged quickly. <a class="wiki-link" href="/people/bixel">bixel</a>'s work on kahu-tok was the central linguistic project of the early tapestry; her student <a class="wiki-link" href="/people/yelena-cruz">yelena cruz</a> completed and formalized the language after bixel's death, across roughly the 2160s to the 2190s. by the middle of the era, kahu-tok was the dominant speech of the community; by the end, it was the only speech.

the philosophical commitments that would define the kahu were established during the tapestry: the principle of <a class="wiki-link" href="/language/kahu"><span class="kt">kahu</span></a> as one word at every scale, the structure of <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/the-council">the council</a>, the early biotech traditions that would mature into <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/the-warden-tenders">the warden-tenders</a>' work, the messenger-scout tradition that would mature into the <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/zou-esh">zou-esh</a>. these did not arrive fully formed. they were worked out across the era through deliberation and practice.

the central outward-facing project of the tapestry was the messenger missions. their geothermal plume from <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/hart-ston">hart-ston</a>, visible from mountain peaks hundreds of miles away on cold days, became known as the mountain that breathes and was noticed by distant survivor pockets. the kahu sent runners out along <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/the-cascade">the cascade</a> arc and beyond, leaving trail markers, carved symbols, and stone cairns. they actively sought out surviving communities and brought back those who could be brought. across the centuries of the tapestry, the kahu absorbed survivor populations from many sources. the descendants of the yamantau bunker in russia, having crossed the frozen bering ice bridge in a multi-generational migration drawn by the legend that had reached them as <span class="proper">tura vyl drak</span>, arrived during the late tapestry. they were not the only arrivals, but they are among the best-documented.

the tapestry ends, by convention, around 2700 ad, when the kahu population, the institutional structures, the language, and the relationship with the outside world had all reached a state that could be called settled. what followed was the <a class="wiki-link" href="/eras/age-of-bloom">age of bloom</a>, the long golden era, which the work of the tapestry established the conditions for.
