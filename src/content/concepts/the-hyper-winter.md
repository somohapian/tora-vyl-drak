the hyper-winter is the kahu term for the years of permanent twilight and freezing temperatures that followed the fracture. it is the climate condition that killed nine in ten of the impact survivors and that defined the conditions of the bunker survival from which the kahu's ancestors emerged.

the hyper-winter began within hours of the impacts of november 10, 2075. the dust columns blown into the upper atmosphere by both fragments, the smoke from regional fires, the ash from secondary destruction, all combined to reduce incoming solar radiation by orders of magnitude. within twenty-four hours, the atmosphere had become a bruised ash-choked shroud. sunlight reached the surface as a dim crimson twilight at the brightest hours of the day.

global temperatures collapsed. agricultural systems that had been operating at scale on the morning of the impact had no chance of producing winter crops at any latitude. the food economy of the pre-collapse world failed within weeks. food wars killed many of the people who had survived the impacts and the immediate tsunamis. cold killed more. starvation finished the rest.

the kahu account of the dark century identifies three waves of extinction across the fracture's aftermath. the impacts and tsunamis were the first. the global famine of the early hyper-winter was the second. the slow grind of environmental poisoning, including the early spread of <span class="kt">peli-shen</span>, was the third. by the time the third wave had concluded, the human population was a fraction of what it had been on impact day.

the hyper-winter lasted long enough to shape the bunker survival strategy entirely. the bunker survivors of the cascade arc lived through years of conditions that made surface emergence impossible. how long the hyper-winter lasted, in precise terms, is open canon. it was long. it lasted, in some readings, into the second generation of bunker life. the slow return of conditions toward something survivable on the surface was one of the things the long watch monitored from the high peaks of the cascade arc as the great eruption's caldera began its long cool.

the hyper-winter ends, in the kahu record, as a slow fade rather than a clean transition. the long watch began when the surface was still impossible and continued for the century and a half during which it became increasingly possible. the kahu cannot date the hyper-winter's end with precision. they can date the moment they decided it was over, which is the first descent in 2283 ad.
