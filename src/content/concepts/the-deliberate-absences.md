the deliberate absences are the words <span class="kt"><a class="wiki-link" href="/language/kahu-tok">kahu-tok</a></span> does not have, and was built not to have. the language has no word for money. none for debt, for rent, for interest. it has <span class="kt">hav</span>, which means to hold, to carry, to have-with-you, and it has no word for <em>own</em>. the gap is not an oversight of a small vocabulary. it is the lexicon's argument, made by silence.

<a class="wiki-link" href="/people/bixel">bixel</a> built a language for a people who survive only together, and a language carries the shape of the thinking it permits. in the old tongues, "family" and "society" were different words, and so the individual could be set against the family, the family against the people, the one against the all. the founder languages also had money, debt, ownership, the whole vocabulary by which one person's having is set against another's. <span class="kt">kahu-tok</span> keeps the first refusal in the word <span class="kt"><a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a></span>, which does not split by scale, and it keeps the second in these absences. you cannot easily say, in kahu-tok, that what you hold is yours against the claim of the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a>, because the language does not give you the word for it.

what the language has instead is a vocabulary of circulation. <span class="kt">giv-giv</span> is to share, the doubled verb of distribution, what a kahu does with a harvest. <span class="kt">trok</span> is to barter, an exchange between equals. <span class="kt">koru-hav</span> is wealth, which parses as what-is-held-for-all; <span class="kt">sin-hav</span> is poverty, which parses as without-holding. even the word for making a wrong right, <span class="kt">nu-du</span>, is the same word as repairing a tool: the kahu do not punish a thief, because there is no theft of what no one owns; they mend what was broken. to hold something is to hold it for the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a>, and the language will not let a speaker forget it.

this is the clearest place the kahu world makes its case against the sovereign individual, and it makes it not as an argument but as a fact of the tongue. a person who wanted to argue for ownership, for accumulation against the community, for the one against the all, would find the words missing from their mouth. to make that case, they would have to make it in another language. to stand outside the kahu, you have to stand outside the language as well.

whether the kahu keep any borrowed terms for these ideas when they deal with the trading world below the rim, whether the absences were bixel's explicit design or simply what the founder peoples already lived, and how the kahu handle money when they must, are open canon.
