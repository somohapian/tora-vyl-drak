<span class="kt">tok-peles</span> is the simplified english-creole patois spoken by the bunker survivors of the dark century. the name in kahu-tok reads place-talk, indicating the language's character as a localized survival speech rather than an inherited formal tongue. the english rendering is the bastard tongue.

the language emerged across the dark century from the contact and simplification of the various pre-collapse languages that the cascade arc bunker survivors brought into their shelters. english was the dominant baseline, but the bunker populations included speakers of other pre-collapse languages, and the technical jargon of the various continuity-of-government, prepper, and rural community installations contributed vocabulary as well. under the conditions of bunker life, formal grammar deteriorated. complex tenses dropped out. vocabulary contracted to what was operationally necessary. by the second or third generation of bunker life, the speech that was being passed to children was substantially different from the english their grandparents had spoken.

<span class="kt">tok-peles</span> was the language of bunker maintenance and shared work. it was adequate for communicating immediate practical needs, transmitting instructions for the operations of bunker life, and maintaining the community across the generations of underground living. it was not suited to the functions kahu-tok would later be designed for: holding philosophical commitments at the level of language, structuring thought around shared community values, and communicating with precision about meaning and intention.

at the first descent, around 2120 ad, and across the early generations of the age of tapestry, <span class="kt">tok-peles</span> was the speech of the founding kahu community. bixel's work on kahu-tok was a deliberate replacement project rather than an incremental evolution, completed and formalized after her death by her student <a class="wiki-link" href="/people/yelena-cruz">yelena cruz</a>. across the tapestry, kahu-tok displaced <span class="kt">tok-peles</span> as the daily language. by the middle of the tapestry, <span class="kt">tok-peles</span> was archaic. by the end of the tapestry, it was a historical artifact preserved in the kahu archive rather than a spoken language.

the kahu's relationship to <span class="kt">tok-peles</span> is respectful and distant. it was the language used by their ancestors through the dark century. it was also the language the kahu set aside when they began to build a different kind of community. the kahu account treats this not as a rejection but as a determination that the bunker-survival speech and the caldera-civilization speech needed to be different languages.
