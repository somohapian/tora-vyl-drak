<span class="kt">tok-peles</span> is the simplified english-creole patois spoken by the bunker survivors of the dark century. the name in kahu-tok reads place-talk, indicating the language's character as a localized survival speech rather than an inherited formal tongue. the english rendering is the bastard tongue.

the language emerged across the dark century from the contact and simplification of the various pre-collapse languages that the cascade arc bunker survivors brought into their shelters. english was the dominant baseline, but the bunker populations included speakers of other pre-collapse languages, and the technical jargon of the various continuity-of-government, prepper, and rural community installations contributed vocabulary as well. under the conditions of bunker life, formal grammar deteriorated. complex tenses dropped out. vocabulary contracted to what was operationally necessary. by the second or third generation of bunker life, the speech that was being passed to children was substantially different from the english their grandparents had spoken.

<span class="kt">tok-peles</span> was the language of bunker maintenance and shared work. it was good for what it had to do: communicate immediate practical needs, transmit instructions for the operations of bunker life, hold the community together across the generations of underground living. it was not good for what kahu-tok would later be designed to do: hold philosophical commitments at the level of language, structure thought around shared community values, communicate with subtlety about meaning and intention.

at the first descent in 2283 ad and across the early generations of the age of tapestry, <span class="kt">tok-peles</span> was the speech of the founding kahu community. bixel's work on kahu-tok was a deliberate replacement project rather than an incremental evolution. across the tapestry, kahu-tok displaced <span class="kt">tok-peles</span> as the daily language. by the middle of the tapestry, <span class="kt">tok-peles</span> was archaic. by the end of the tapestry, it was a historical artifact preserved in the kahu archive rather than a spoken language.

the kahu's relationship to <span class="kt">tok-peles</span> is respectful and distant. it was the language that carried their ancestors through the dark century. it was also the language that the kahu chose to leave behind when they began to build a different kind of community. the choice was not a rejection. it was a recognition that the bunker-survival speech and the caldera-civilization speech were going to need to be different languages.
