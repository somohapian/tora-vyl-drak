the bixel 1000 is the engineered core of the kahu-tok language: roughly three hundred fifty working roots, ninety of them kept from the old languages, with compounds and set phrases carrying the count to the thousand. it is bixel's deliberate design from the early age of tapestry, the architecture of the language at the lexical level. in kahu-tok the founding lexicon is called the <a class="wiki-link" href="/language/mil-tok">mil-tok</a>, the thousand words; the bixel 1000 and the mil-tok are the same body of words under two names, one the design story and one what a kahu calls it. the dictionary of kahu-tok divides between the bixel 1000 (engineered roots) and the organic accretions (words like <span class="kt">micasu</span> that entered the lexicon by use over the centuries that followed).

bixel's work on the bixel 1000 was the central linguistic project of the early tapestry. the goal was a vocabulary large enough to handle the practical and philosophical needs of the kahu community, small enough to be learnable and teachable across generations, and architecturally consistent enough to support productive compounding (the way <span class="kt">peli</span> and <span class="kt">shen</span> combine into <span class="kt">peli-shen</span>, or the way <span class="kt">ston</span> appears in <span class="kt">ston-shen</span>, <span class="kt">ston-ropa</span>, and <span class="proper">ston-klimb</span>). most of kahu-tok's actual vocabulary in daily use consists of bixel 1000 roots and the compounds built from them.

the bixel 1000 is not closed. across the centuries since bixel's work, the kahu have continued to formalize new roots, refine existing ones, and occasionally retire roots that have lost their utility. the process is open canon in its details. the institutional home of this work is unclear; it may sit with the warden-tenders, or with a dedicated linguistic order, or be distributed across the kahu's scholarly traditions. the modern bixel 1000 carries bixel's name as a tradition rather than a static archive.

the count is settled in the mil-tok's own coda: roughly three hundred fifty working roots, some ninety of them the old shared words bixel only regularized, and the compounds, doublings, and set phrases that grow from them carrying the count to the thousand. the inventory of which roots are formally in at any given moment is the kind of question the kahu's linguistic archive holds answers to. for the purposes of the present wiki, specific roots are documented as individual entries as they are confirmed.

the bixel 1000 is also the source of the language's distinctive structural commitments. importance-first syntax is built into how the roots combine. the principle that <span class="kt">kahu</span> is one word at every scale is implemented at the lexical level by the choice not to provide alternative roots for "family" and "society." bixel's design decisions operate at the level of the language's mechanics. the roots encode the philosophical commitments of the kahu civilization in the everyday units of speech.
