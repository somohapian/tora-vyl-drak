the three waves of extinction is the kahu framework for understanding how the human population collapsed across the dark century. the kahu's account of the fracture's aftermath identifies three distinct waves, each of which killed a substantial fraction of the survivors of the previous wave.

**the first wave: impact and immediate destruction.** the comet fragments and their consequences on the day of the fracture. the atmospheric blasts. the tsunamis from the atlantic and cascadia impacts. the magnitude nine or greater megathrust earthquake along the juan de fuca plate. the structural collapses that rippled across eurasia from the atlantic impact and across the americas from the cascadia impact. approximately half the global population died in the first wave, mostly in the first twenty-four hours.

**the second wave: famine and the hyper-winter.** the years of permanent twilight and freezing temperatures that followed the impacts. atmospheric dust and smoke reduced incoming solar radiation by orders of magnitude. agricultural systems failed within weeks. food wars killed many of the survivors of the first wave; cold and starvation killed most of the rest. the food economy of the pre-collapse world simply did not function under hyper-winter conditions, and the populations that had survived the impacts had no time to develop alternatives. approximately nine in ten of the first-wave survivors died in the second wave.

**the third wave: poisoning and the slow grind.** the long stretch of environmental contamination that followed the immediate hyper-winter. heavy-metal fallout from the comet's iron, nickel, iridium, platinum, and radioactive content. the rise of <span class="kt">peli-shen</span> from native fungal lineages, catalyzed by the heavy metals and the hard radiation, gradually claiming the lowlands. radiation sickness across exposed regions. the breakdown of medical care without pre-collapse pharmaceutical infrastructure. the third wave killed across decades rather than days, and its exact mortality is harder to pin down, but the human population that emerged into the late dark century was a small fraction of what had survived the second wave.

the framework represents the dark century as a sequence of distinct events rather than a single one. each wave killed a different population through a different mechanism, so the survivors of all three waves were the survivors of three distinct selection pressures in succession. the kahu's account of their own existence notes that their ancestors survived three separate selection events, by different mechanisms, across the better part of two centuries.

the three-waves framework also functions as the kahu's caution about catastrophe. survival of the first wave does not confer safety from the second or third. resilience to one kind of crisis is not resilience to the kinds of crisis that follow. the kahu's institutional structure, including its reserves, its biotech infrastructure, and its management of the systems that sustain daily life, is shaped by the recognition that catastrophe can recur from new directions over long periods.
