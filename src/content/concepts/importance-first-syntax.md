importance-first syntax is the foundational syntactic principle of kahu-tok. the most important element of a sentence leads. emphasis and focus are marked by position from the first word.

the principle is structural rather than stylistic. it is built into the language's grammar. when a kahu speaker constructs a sentence, the leading position is occupied by whatever element of the utterance carries the most weight in the current context. that element may be the subject, the object, the verb, a temporal marker, or a modifier, depending on the content of the sentence. the choice of leading element carries information in addition to the content of the sentence.

this differs from the english pattern, in which fixed word order (subject-verb-object) means that the most important element of an english sentence is typically in the middle and is inferred from emphasis, intonation, or surrounding context. in kahu-tok, the most important element is marked by position rather than inferred.

the principle has consequences for kahu social life. a speaker has fewer grammatical means of obscuring the intended emphasis of an utterance. a kahu listener can identify the speaker's main point before the utterance is complete. discussions proceed more quickly because the emphasis of each contribution is marked from its opening words.

the name <span class="proper">tora vyl drak</span> is an example of importance-first construction at the level of a phrase. the words read hope, call, last in their kahu-tok order. the most important element, hope, leads. the qualification, that it is the call of the last attempt, follows. the english renderings "the hope of the last call" or "the last call's hope" move the important element to a position that an english listener parses as the head. the kahu construction places the important element first.

bixel's design of kahu-tok was guided by the principle that the language should not allow its speakers to confuse themselves or each other about intended meaning. importance-first syntax is one of the tools bixel used to implement that principle. the other principal tool is the refusal to encode scale distinctions in the word <span class="kt">kahu</span>. these two design decisions affect most kahu utterances.
