the great devaluation is the kahu term for the decades of institutional decay, information warfare, and environmental degradation that preceded the fracture. it is, in kahu reckoning, the world that broke before the world broke.

the devaluation is understood by the kahu as the precondition for everything that followed. an asteroid impact happens to a civilization. what kind of civilization it is at the moment of impact shapes the survival, the recovery, and the long aftermath. the kahu's reading of the historical record is that humanity's response to the fracture, the volley that cleaved the comet, the leveling that sealed the longs in, the rise of the kahu themselves, was shaped by the kind of civilization that 2075 found.

the devaluation spans, in approximate terms, the decades of the 2040s through the moment of the fracture in 2075. its features are recorded in the kahu archive in summary form. intense information warfare, ai-driven hyper-propaganda saturating public discourse, institutional trust collapses across governments and media and the professions, accelerating environmental degradation as ecological systems failed faster than political systems could respond. the population was numerically large and educated but unable to act coherently on the threats it could see coming.

the central technological event of the devaluation was the miskinnis discovery of biological immortality, traced to the work of hanna miskinnis and her research group, completed in the period around 2051 to 2062 ad. the kahu read this discovery as the moment when the devaluation had a chance to become something else. instead, the technology was monopolized by the ultra-wealthy. the longs took it behind walls. the rest of humanity got nothing, and the devaluation deepened. hanna refused to use her own work; the lineage of refusal that descends from her decision is the kahu purists' founding precedent.

the kahu do not romanticize the pre-collapse world. their archive treats the devaluation as a cautionary case, the world that was unable to save itself, the civilization whose breakage was already partly accomplished before the comet finished it. the kahu's institutions are built, in part, against the failure modes of the devaluation. the council's structure, the warden-tenders' practice, the language's refusal of certain conceptual moves: all of it reads in part as a deliberate refusal to repeat the devaluation's mistakes.
