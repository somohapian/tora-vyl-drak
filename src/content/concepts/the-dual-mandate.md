the dual mandate is the formal shape of the <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/zou-esh">zou-esh</a> mission. the order's work in the world below has two halves, and the order names them as two.

the first half is the search. it is the sacred duty: finding <span class="kt">nu-pipo</span>, the survivors who might be brought home through <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/the-gate">the gate</a>, and scavenging <span class="kt"><a class="wiki-link" href="/language/ol-hart">ol-hart</a></span>, the vital resources and technology the old world left behind. the search is the half of the mandate that the rest of the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a> understand most readily, because its returns are people and materials, things that come back up <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/the-long-climb">the long climb</a> and join the life of the caldera.

the second half is the study. it is the scientific work: the ecological and archaeological data-gathering that builds the kahu's picture of what the world outside the sanctuary has become. how far the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/peli-shen">peli-shen</a> has spread and where its limits hold, what has happened to the ruined cities, what survives and what does not, how the climate and the land are changing across the long centuries. the study returns knowledge rather than goods, and that knowledge feeds the modeling of <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/warden">warden</a> and the long planning of the kahu.

the two halves are not separable in practice. a zou-esh in the field watches, judges, and decides under the search mandate, and records, measures, and remembers under the study mandate, often in the same hour. the order is a scholar-warrior order, and the dual mandate is where the scholar and the warrior meet. the warrior carries the search. the scholar carries the study. one zou-esh holds both.

the precise kahu-tok terms for the two halves are open canon. the search is sometimes rendered as the vyl, the study as the no, but whether these are exactly right is not settled.
