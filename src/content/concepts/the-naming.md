every <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a> carries one name, and no two living kahu carry the same one.

the mononym is as old as the language. in the founding years, new entrants could not hear where words began and ended in tongues they did not speak, so <a class="wiki-link" href="/people/bixel">bixel</a> encouraged learning a person's whole spoken name as a single label. a survivor introduced as mike ross became mikeross, one sound, one person. the practice held. founder populations clustered by origin, and their name-stocks clustered with them, which is why a kahu can usually place a stranger's region of the caldera by name-type alone, and sometimes the kahu itself. the fused names of several founder peoples give tens of millions of possible forms against a living population of some two hundred and seventy-eight thousand, so uniqueness costs nothing and lasts indefinitely.

a name is given a few days after birth, never at it. before any name is spoken, everyone in the kahu wants to meet the child, hold the child, experience the child, and a kahu is a large household, so that takes time. only then does the kahu go together to the <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/the-warden-tenders">warden-tender</a> to learn which names are free and work through them until one answers. the registry held by <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/warden">warden</a> guarantees that the chosen name belongs to no other living kahu. who speaks it first differs hearth to hearth. very few kahu have a leader or a matriarch, and the order of things at a naming is local custom. the delay is practical, not ritual: the name answers the child, and the kahu meets the child first.

the naming is a celebration. the older members of the kahu tend the child and the other children while the younger members head to <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/hot-kasa">hot-kasa</a>. from life comes life. clean algae-grown intoxicants are part of the evening, gentle ones, happy rather than wild.

the registry name is the public self, the one the archive knows. inside the kahu a person is called by fragments and hearth-names, mike or ross or whatever the family settled on, and those are plural, shifting, and owned by no one, like everything else in a kahu.

at death a name returns to the pool. what happens next varies by kahu. some let it rest. the purists pass it on, a practice with its own custom, the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/the-rebirth-naming">rebirth naming</a>. a small number of names never return at all: the <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/the-council">council</a> can retire a name permanently, on the model of a sainthood or a retired storm-name, and the great names of the founding and reforming generations, <span class="proper">mara</span>, <span class="proper">ston</span>, <span class="proper">bixel</span>, are held that way, unavailable to the living.
