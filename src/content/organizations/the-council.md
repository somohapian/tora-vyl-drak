the council is the central institutional structure of kahu governance. it is the body that deliberates on caldera-wide questions and integrates the recommendations of warden into kahu decision-making.

the council does not rule in the sense that a pre-collapse state ruled. the kahu's governance is mostly at scale; daily life is managed at the level of the small kahu (family unit), the larger kahu (sub-community), and the working kahu of vocational groups. most decisions a kahu makes in a day are made at these scales without reference to any central body. the council exists for the decisions that cannot be made at smaller scales: caldera-wide resource allocation, infrastructure investment, the integration of warden's modeling outputs into actual practice, the response to events that affect the entire community (the caldera shudder being the most recent example), and the formal mediation of disagreements that cannot be resolved within the kahu they arose in.

the council sits in hot-kasa, in chambers within the principal settlement. its precise composition and selection process are open canon. the kahu reference to the council uses the singular: there is one council, it does its work continuously, its deliberations are public in the kahu sense (any kahu may attend) without being broadcast or recorded in a pre-collapse public-meeting sense.

the council consults warden as the modeling layer for any decision warden can usefully inform. agricultural allocation, infrastructure investment, demographic projection, ecological signals. warden's recommendations are input, not verdict. a council decision contrary to warden is rare but is not forbidden. the council holds the deciding role. warden holds the modeling role. the warden-tenders maintain warden. the relationships are clean by design.

the council's authority does not extend into kahu vocational life in a directive sense. the zou-esh determine their own internal practices, with the council weighing in only when zou-esh activity affects the broader caldera (which is most missions, in practice, but the form of the involvement is consultation rather than command). the cat-kahu, the warden-tenders, the agricultural sub-communities, the medical orders, all manage their own internal work. the council is the integrating body, not the controlling one.

the council's relationship to the schism, the unfolding kahu disagreement about lifespan and intervention, is one of formal neutrality. council members are kahu and hold their own positions, but the council as a body does not have a position. its role in the schism is to keep the disagreement productive rather than destructive. whether it will be able to do this indefinitely is open.
