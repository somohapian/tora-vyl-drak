among the two hundred seventy-eight thousand there is one family whose children learn their letters before their chores. the buk-kahu are the librarians of tora vyl drak: a single kahu, twenty to forty people in any generation, who hold the memo-kasa and everything in it. the library is their charge the way the cats are the cat-kahu's charge and the warden is the warden-tenders'. no one appointed them and no law compels them. the charge is held the way the kahu hold everything that matters, by a family that took it up and never put it down.

the heart of the holding is the tok-kahu, the dictionary of the people's tongue. the buk-kahu keep the four editions of the mil-tok, bixel's own document, and the cruz redaction beside them, and around that core they keep the ledger of everything the language has done since: the accretions, the kept sounds, the eponyms, the phrases worn smooth by use. when a word's sense moves, they mark the movement. when the council retires a name into honor, they record the retirement, and when a name is left in the common pool unremarked, they record that too, and the silence around it. the drift-charts are their long work: century against century, the same hundred words traced like riverbeds, showing where the tongue has wandered and where it has held.

folk memory says the charge began in yelena cruz's household. the record cannot confirm it and the buk-kahu do not claim it, which the kahu take as a kind of claim. the story runs that when cruz died and the age forgot her, her household kept her working papers because no one else wanted them, and the family that kept the carpenter's papers became, in time, the family that kept all papers. whatever the truth, the buk-kahu keep her redaction with a care that exceeds curatorship.

between the buk-kahu and the warden-tenders runs the oldest quiet rivalry in the caldera, because the kahu keep two memories. the warden holds the names: the living registry, the record that breathes. the buk-kahu hold the words: the paper record, the one that does not change when no one is looking. the tenders say paper burns. the librarians say tissue forgets. both are right, which is why there are two. the proverb belongs to the librarians: buk sav mor, buk tok net. the book knows more and says nothing.
