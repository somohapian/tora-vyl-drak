the three speakers are the executive triad of <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a> governance, the small body that articulates the consensus of the two chambers of <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/the-council-of-the-tapestry">the council of the tapestry</a> and gives it a voice.

the first speaker is the voice of the people's council, the lower chamber, the will of the living community. the elder speaker is the voice of the council of watchers, the upper chamber of retired <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/zou-esh">zou-esh</a> who hold the long survival horizon. between them stands the weaver.

the weaver is the neutral one. the weaver belongs to neither chamber and speaks for neither interest. chosen jointly by both chambers, the weaver's task is to find and articulate the consensus between the present and the long horizon, between what the people want and what survival allows. the title is exact. the kahu name their whole civilization after the tapestry, the <a class="wiki-link" href="/eras/age-of-tapestry">age of tapestry</a> being the era in which they wove themselves back into a people, and the weaver is the one who weaves the two chambers into a single thread of decision.

the triad is the kahu refusal of a single ruler made into a working office. no one speaks alone. the present has a voice, the survival horizon has a voice, and the consensus between them has a voice, and the three are different people. the structure makes it hard for any one interest to capture the executive, which is the point.

the kahu-tok titles of the three speakers, how each is selected, and what the weaver can do when the two chambers will not agree are open canon.
