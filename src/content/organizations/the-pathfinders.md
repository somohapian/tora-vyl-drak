the pathfinders are the judicial council of the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a>, known in kahu-tok as <span class="kt">tru-tapi</span>. there are three of them, drawn from three different parts of kahu life so that no single perspective holds the whole of justice.

one pathfinder is a retired <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/zou-esh">zou-esh</a> elder, bringing the order's hard knowledge of threat and protection. one is a miskinnis purist elder, bringing the tradition that holds the kahu's deepest commitments about biotech and dependence. one is a master cultivator, bringing the agricultural and communal grounding of everyday kahu life. the three together are meant to hold the full weight of a judgment, the threshold, the principle, and the field.

kahu justice is restorative and protective in focus. its aim is to repair what was broken and to protect the community, not to punish for its own sake. the pathfinders do not impose death. the final penalty in the kahu system is exile, called <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/the-last-walk">the last walk</a>. the condemned is escorted by zou-esh beyond the rim and out of <span class="proper"><a class="wiki-link" href="/places/tora-vyl-drak">tora vyl drak</a></span>, given tools, seeds, and knowledge, and left to live or die in the world below. it is the gravest thing the kahu do to one of their own, and it stops short of killing.

the refusal to impose death is consistent with the kahu's whole moral architecture. a people who define existence as existence-within-the-kahu treat removal from the kahu as the ultimate penalty, more total than death in the terms of their own philosophy, since to be outside the kahu is, in the logic of their language, to no longer be. they do not need to kill. they need only to send someone out.

how the pathfinders are chosen, the full range of lesser penalties available to them, and how often the last walk has actually been imposed are open canon.
