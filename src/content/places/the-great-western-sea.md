the great western sea is the toxic inland sea created by the 2075 cascadia impact. it sits west of the cascade arc, where the willamette valley used to be. it is one of the major geographic features of the kahu's world and the western boundary of the territory the zou-esh operate within.

the sea formed in the hours of the fracture. the four-kilometer cascadia fragment of the comet impacted the juan de fuca plate off the oregon coast. the magnitude nine or greater megathrust earthquake that followed liquefied the pacific northwest's coastal cities and caused immediate large-scale coastal subsidence. the pacific roared inland through the new low ground, flooding the willamette valley and adjacent lowlands across what is now a substantial inland sea.

the water is contaminated. heavy-metal fallout from the impact itself, the wreckage of the pre-collapse coastal cities and industrial infrastructure now submerged, and ongoing sediment loading from the regional environmental conditions all keep the sea outside the range of normal marine biology. whether any organisms have adapted to the conditions and what those organisms might be is open canon.

the sea is a barrier and a boundary for the kahu. it sits below the <span class="kt">peli-shen</span> spore line, which is also the case for everything west of the cascade arc, so its toxicity is doubled by the conditions of the air above it. zou-esh expeditions reaching the sea's edge from the cascade arc are operating in the most hostile environment the kahu's missions encounter. the practical value of reaching the sea is limited; the kahu's geographic interest in the region is observational rather than operational.

the kahu's view from the rim of <span class="proper">tora vyl drak</span> on the clearest days includes the haze that hangs over the great western sea. the sea is visible as a flat dim band on the western horizon. its existence is one of the kahu's everyday confirmations that the world they live in is the world the fracture made.
