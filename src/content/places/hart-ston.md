<span class="proper">hart-ston</span> is the active volcanic cone that sits within the caldera. the name in kahu-tok reads heart-stone. the cone is sacred to the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a>. it is also the source of the residual heat that keeps them alive.

the underlying volcano of <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/tora-vyl-drak">tora vyl drak</a> is mostly dead but slightly alive. hart-ston is the place where the slightly alive is visible. the cone vents steam on cold days, a continuous plume that on freezing winter mornings rose tall enough in the centuries after <a class="wiki-link" href="/events/the-first-descent">the first descent</a> to be seen from mountain peaks hundreds of miles away. this is the mountain that breathes, the beacon by which distant survivor pockets first knew that something in the cascades was warm.

the kahu's relationship to hart-ston is the relationship of a people to the heat their lives depend on. <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/the-warden-tenders">the warden-tenders</a> maintain its perimeter and observe its signs. the cone is approached but not lived on. its sacredness is functional rather than mystical: this is the part of the mountain that is still alive, and what is still alive deserves the consideration the kahu give to anything that holds the conditions for life.

<a class="wiki-link" href="/events/the-caldera-shudder">the caldera shudder</a> of approximately 4525 ad was a seismic warning from beneath the caldera. whether hart-ston was its origin point or merely registered it more visibly than other parts of the basin is open canon.
