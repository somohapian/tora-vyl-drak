<span class="proper">hart-ston</span> is the active volcanic cone that sits within the caldera. the name in kahu-tok reads heart-stone. the cone is sacred to the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a>. it is also the source of the residual heat that keeps them alive.

the underlying volcano of <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/tora-vyl-drak">tora vyl drak</a> is mostly dead but slightly alive. hart-ston is the place where the remaining activity is visible. the cone vents steam on cold days. in the centuries after <a class="wiki-link" href="/events/the-first-descent">the first descent</a>, on freezing winter mornings, the plume rose tall enough to be seen from mountain peaks hundreds of miles away. distant survivor pockets used it as a marker that something in the cascades was warm.

the kahu depend on hart-ston for heat. <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/the-warden-tenders">the warden-tenders</a> maintain its perimeter and observe its signs. the cone is approached but not lived on. its sacredness is functional rather than mystical: it is the part of the mountain that is still active.

<a class="wiki-link" href="/events/the-caldera-shudder">the caldera shudder</a> of approximately 4525 ad was a seismic event beneath the caldera. whether hart-ston was its origin point or registered it more visibly than other parts of the basin is open canon.
