<span class="proper">hot-kasa</span> is the principal settlement of the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a>. the name in kahu-tok reads hot-house, the warmth. approximately one hundred and twenty thousand kahu live here, roughly forty-three percent of the total population.

the settlement is built into the rock of <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/the-hearth">the hearth</a>, the west quadrant of the caldera, where the residual magma chamber is closest to the surface. the kahu live in chambers carved through natural lava tubes, lava flow voids, and additional tunneling, a high-density three-dimensional city in the rock. the geothermal grid runs hot enough through the hearth that the chambers do not require additional heating in winter. proximity to the magma chamber is the reason hot-kasa is hot-kasa.

private chambers are modest. communal spaces are not. the kahu pattern of shared living follows the philosophical commitment to the kahu as the unit of human life, the small family kahu and the larger sub-community kahu and the entire two hundred and seventy-eight thousand kahu all the same word at every scale. cooking, eating, gathering, and most working life happen in the communal spaces. the small chambers are for sleeping and the private hours.

the volume of the mountain is far larger than the settlement currently occupies. estimates suggest hot-kasa uses approximately one quarter of the carved-space capacity available along the rim. the kahu have not pressed against the structural limits of the settlement. population growth has room to expand without leaving the rock.

hot-kasa is the home of <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/the-council">the council</a>, the central institutional structure of kahu life, and of the terminals through which <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/warden">warden</a> is consulted. the seat of the council and the principal warden terminal are within the settlement; the precise architectural locations are open canon.
