the long climb, <span class="kt">klimb-long</span> in kahu-tok, is the narrow ascent path from the lowlands to the <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/nu-tera">nu-tera</a> plateau. the mil-tok uses the name as its compounding example: the climb leads, and the length qualifies it. it is the only land approach to <span class="proper"><a class="wiki-link" href="/places/tora-vyl-drak">tora vyl drak</a></span> from the world below the <span class="kt"><a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/peli-shen">peli-shen</a></span> spore line. anyone who reaches the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a> reaches them by this route, <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/the-gate">the gate</a> at its top, and the stone climb above that.

the climb runs up the southeastern flank of <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/the-cascade">the cascade</a> arc, gaining roughly two thousand feet of elevation over its length. the route is consistently narrow. in places it tightens to passages where one person at a time is the rule. the surface is uneven, the footing in many sections is rock that requires watching, and the elevation gain is sustained rather than gradual. the climb takes most travelers a full day under good conditions and longer under any conditions worse than good.

the climb is impassable in winter. snow accumulates at the higher elevations and the lower switchbacks ice over. passes that look open from below close, and weather windows narrow. attempts to ascend in winter, when survivors below have made them, have usually failed. the kahu's summer-only operating season for <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/zou-esh">zou-esh</a> missions is in part a consequence of the climb's seasonal limit.

the gate sits at the top of the long climb where the rock opens out onto the nu-tera plateau. the chokepoint geography is what makes the gate the defensible point it is. there is no other way up onto the plateau that does not first require a much longer climb up other parts of the rim, through terrain that is harder to cross than the long climb itself.

<a class="wiki-link" href="/people/mara">mara</a> died at the gate, defending the top of the long climb against raiders who had come up it during summer season. the geography of the climb is the reason her position was defensible by one person, since a single defender can hold a chokepoint that many people cannot pass through abreast.
