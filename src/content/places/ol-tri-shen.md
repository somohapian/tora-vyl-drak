<span class="proper">ol-tri shen</span> is the forest preserve at the heart of <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/the-wilds">the wilds</a>, the north quadrant of <span class="proper"><a class="wiki-link" href="/places/tora-vyl-drak">tora vyl drak</a></span>. the name in kahu-tok reads old-tree life. it is the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a>'s longest-running deliberate non-cultivation and the closest thing inside the caldera to ancient ecology.

nothing inside the caldera predates 2075, the year of <a class="wiki-link" href="/events/the-great-eruption">the great eruption</a> that formed the basin. ol-tri shen does not predate that year either. what makes it the old-tree forest is that, since the early generations of the <a class="wiki-link" href="/eras/age-of-tapestry">age of tapestry</a>, the kahu have allowed it to grow without instruction. <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/the-warden-tenders">the warden-tenders</a> do not direct its succession. <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/the-council">the council</a> does not allocate its productivity. <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/the-cat-kahu">the cat-kahu</a> pass through with discretion. otherwise, the forest is left alone.

what has grown there is the result of long uninterrupted ecological process. species composition, canopy structure, mycorrhizal networks, and soil chemistry have all developed without kahu interference for the bulk of two thousand years.

the kahu enter ol-tri shen for observation, for retreat, and occasionally as part of <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/zou-esh">zou-esh</a> training. the rules of entry are oral and shared rather than codified. they reduce to: take nothing that is alive, leave nothing that did not come with you, and walk where you can walk without breaking what is growing. those who want to know the forest learn it over many visits.

ol-tri shen is the result of the kahu deliberately not managing it.
