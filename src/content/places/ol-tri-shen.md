<span class="proper">ol-tri shen</span> is the forest preserve at the heart of the wilds, the north quadrant of <span class="proper">tora vyl drak</span>. the name in kahu-tok reads old-tree life. it is the kahu's longest-running deliberate non-cultivation and the closest thing inside the caldera to ancient ecology.

nothing inside the caldera predates 2133 ad, the year of the great eruption that formed the basin. ol-tri shen does not predate that year either. what makes it the old-tree forest is that, since the early generations of the age of tapestry, the kahu have allowed it to grow without instruction. the warden-tenders do not direct its succession. the council does not allocate its productivity. the cat-kahu pass through with discretion. otherwise, the forest is left alone.

what has grown there is the result of long uninterrupted ecological process. species composition, canopy structure, mycorrhizal networks, soil chemistry, all have developed without kahu interference for the bulk of two thousand years. the forest is not a museum. it is the practice of letting something proceed on its own terms.

the kahu enter ol-tri shen for observation, for retreat, and occasionally as part of zou-esh training. the rules of entry are oral and shared, not codified. they reduce to: take nothing that is alive, leave nothing that did not come with you, walk where you can walk without breaking what is growing. those who want to know the forest learn it slowly and over many visits.

ol-tri shen is one of the things the kahu have made by not making it. its meaning to the community is roughly that.
