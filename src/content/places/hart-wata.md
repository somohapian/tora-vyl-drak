<span class="proper">hart-wata</span> is the great lake at the center of <span class="proper">tora vyl drak</span>. the name in kahu-tok reads heart-water. it is the kahu's primary freshwater source and the geographic center of life inside the caldera.

the lake formed in the aftermath of the great eruption, fed by snowmelt from the rim and by groundwater seepage from the chambers below. its waters are clear, cold, and continuously replenished. the lake does not freeze in winter, owing to the residual geothermal warmth of the caldera basin.

hart-wata sits roughly at the center of the eighteen-mile caldera. the four quadrants of the basin organize around it: the hearth to the west, sun-tera to the south, the wilds to the north, and the transitional eastern approach where the stone climb arrives from the nu-tera plateau. the lake is the reference point against which the geography of <span class="proper">tora vyl drak</span> is described.

kahu use of the lake is everyday and total. drinking water, agricultural water, the freshwater fisheries that supplement kahu diet, and the cooling and humidity regulation of the chambers all run off hart-wata. the warden-tenders maintain the systems that draw from and return to the lake without degrading its quality. the lake is one of the central living systems of the caldera, and its care is one of the order's principal responsibilities.
