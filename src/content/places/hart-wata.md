<span class="proper">hart-wata</span> is the great lake at the center of <span class="proper"><a class="wiki-link" href="/places/tora-vyl-drak">tora vyl drak</a></span>. the name in kahu-tok reads heart-water. it is the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a>'s primary freshwater source and the geographic center of life inside the caldera.

the lake formed in the aftermath of <a class="wiki-link" href="/events/the-great-eruption">the great eruption</a>, fed by snowmelt from the rim and by groundwater seepage from the chambers below. its waters are clear, cold, and continuously replenished. the lake does not freeze in winter, owing to the residual geothermal warmth of the caldera basin.

hart-wata sits roughly at the center of the eighteen-mile caldera. the four quadrants of the basin organize around it: <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/the-hearth">the hearth</a> to the west, <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/sun-tera">sun-tera</a> to the south, <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/the-wilds">the wilds</a> to the north, and the transitional eastern approach where the stone climb arrives from the <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/nu-tera">nu-tera</a> plateau. the lake is the reference point against which the geography of <span class="proper">tora vyl drak</span> is described.

the kahu use the lake extensively. drinking water, agricultural water, the freshwater fisheries that supplement kahu diet, and the cooling and humidity regulation of the chambers all run off hart-wata. <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/the-warden-tenders">the warden-tenders</a> maintain the systems that draw from and return to the lake without degrading its quality. its care is one of the order's principal responsibilities.
