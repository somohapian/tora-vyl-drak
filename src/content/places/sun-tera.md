<span class="proper">sun-tera</span> is the south quadrant of the caldera. the name in kahu-tok reads sun-land. it is the kahu's principal agricultural zone inside <span class="proper">tora vyl drak</span> proper and the source of most of the caldera's food.

the south quadrant receives the most direct sunlight available within the basin, which the rim shadows on three sides for parts of each day. soil quality is high; the post-eruption sterilization of the caldera floor in 2133 ad laid down nutrient-rich volcanic ash that has since been built up further by centuries of kahu agricultural practice. the river called komi-wata runs through the quadrant, feeding the lower fields and joining hart-wata at the basin center.

sun-tera produces grain, root crops, vegetables, and the fruits and oilseeds that round out the kahu diet. cultivation is organized around the warden-tenders' recommendations and the council's allocation decisions, but most actual labor is done by kahu families and sub-communities working their assigned fields. the work is rotating and shared rather than fixed by family.

sun-tera was temporarily reduced in productivity by the caldera shudder of approximately 4525 ad. some fields lost growing season to repair work. the quadrant has since recovered to or near its pre-shudder output. the kahu's caloric surplus is large enough that even the temporary reduction did not produce hunger; what it produced was the kahu's first lived experience of provisional stability since the age of bloom.

the agricultural belt of nu-tera, on the plateau below the rim, runs the same fundamental crops with adjustments for the slightly cooler and more exposed conditions. together, sun-tera and nu-tera produce the kahu food economy. the relationship between the two zones is collaborative, with rotation of methods and seeds between them.
