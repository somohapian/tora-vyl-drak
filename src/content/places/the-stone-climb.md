<span class="kt">ston-klimb</span> is the spiral walkway cut into the rock face that connects the nu-tera plateau to the caldera floor of <span class="proper">tora vyl drak</span>. the english rendering is the stone climb. its name in kahu-tok reads stone-climb, directly. the walkway rises approximately eight hundred feet of elevation from the plateau at five thousand two hundred feet to the caldera floor at six thousand.

the stone climb is the second of the two ascents into <span class="proper">tora vyl drak</span>. the long climb runs from the lowlands up to the nu-tera plateau, gaining roughly two thousand feet across a narrow natural route that is impassable in winter. the stone climb runs from the plateau up to the caldera floor, gaining the remaining eight hundred feet through a carved spiral that the kahu built into the rock face.

the carving was done across early generations of the age of tapestry, when the founding kahu were establishing the basic infrastructure of caldera life. the spiral takes the form of a ramp wide enough for two-way pedestrian traffic and pack-animal passage but narrow enough to be defensible at multiple points. it is structurally sound and has been maintained continuously since its completion. it does not become impassable in winter the way the long climb does, but it requires care in icy conditions and is subject to caldera-rim weather effects.

the stone climb is what makes the integrated kahu economy possible. agricultural output from nu-tera moves up to the caldera floor along it, and specialty goods and biotech products from hot-kasa move down to nu-tera along it. people use the stone climb daily in both directions, for visits, for work, and for regular movement between plateau communities and caldera communities. the climb is the principal route of internal kahu travel.

the stone climb is also where the standard kahu-tok phrase comes from, the one used to invite a newcomer farther into <span class="proper">tora vyl drak</span>. an invitation to make the stone climb is an invitation to the caldera floor. it refers to the literal movement that the invitation represents.
