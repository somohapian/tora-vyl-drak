the bunker survivors are the populations that rode out the dark century underground in the deep mountain installations of the cascade region. the largest of them were among the founding groups of the kahu; the smaller, scattered bunkers contributed their survivors later. the kahu descend in part from these populations.

the founding bunkers were the major installations. cheyenne mountain and the united states government continuity bunkers carried the surviving apparatus of the pre-collapse american state between them. a separate "elites and talented" bunker, built and populated by a pre-collapse billionaire when the comet was detected, carried a third. these reached the caldera in <a class="wiki-link" href="/events/the-first-descent">the first descent</a> and held seats on the first council. the smaller bunkers of preppers and rural communities, scattered through the idaho rockies and the blue mountains of eastern oregon, were not part of the founding; their survivors filtered into the kahu over the centuries that followed, found by <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/zou-esh">zou-esh</a> missions, arriving at <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/the-gate">the gate</a>, or encountered as raiders. none of these were the <a class="wiki-link" href="/people/the-longs">longs</a>' arcologies.

bunker life across the dark century was, in the kahu's own account, structured by the failure curve of pre-collapse technology and the parallel rise of biological adaptation. the engineering inheritance of the bunker generation gave way within decades; the social inheritance frayed across the generation that followed. children of bunker-life were not the same people their bunker-builder ancestors had been. by the founding, around 2120 ad, the populations that descended were demographically and culturally distinct from the populations that had entered.

what they had developed underground that they brought with them was substantial. <span class="kt">ston-shen</span>, the chemosynthetic lichen-bacteria paste cultivated on geothermal vents, was the food technology that made survival possible past the failure of the rations. the bastard tongue, tok-peles, was the speech they shared. early fungiculture and underground livestock practices that would mature into kahu agriculture were established. small-community living patterns under conditions of resource scarcity were practiced across many generations; the kahu's communal living patterns are direct inheritance from bunker life.

the different bunker populations were not identical. the prepper bunkers, the rural community shelters, and the continuity-of-government installations carried different cultural baselines from their pre-collapse origins. across the dark century these distinctions softened under the shared conditions of survival, but they did not entirely disappear. the founding generation of the kahu was a composite community that took the centuries of the age of tapestry to fully integrate into a single culture.

the bunker survivors do not appear in the kahu record as named individuals except in fragments. the documentation tradition that the kahu would later develop did not exist underground. what is known of the bunker survivors comes from oral traditions preserved into the tapestry, from later archeological recovery of bunker sites by zou-esh expeditions, and from inferences drawn by kahu historians working with the materials available. most of what these populations lived through has been lost.
