bixel built the kahu-tok language. the work began in the early generations after <a class="wiki-link" href="/events/the-first-descent">the first descent</a>, when the survivors who had carried the bastard tongue down out of the bunkers needed a shared speech that could grow with the new community.

the language bixel designed has two organizing principles. importance-first syntax places the most important element of a sentence at its head, so that the speaker's emphasis is visible from the first word. and the bixel 1000, the engineered core vocabulary of approximately one thousand roots and stems, gives kahu-tok its baseline. on top of the bixel 1000, organic accretions from the bunker populations the <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/zou-esh">zou-esh</a> brought home have entered the language by use rather than by design. words like <span class="kt"><a class="wiki-link" href="/language/micasu">micasu</a></span> arrived from spanish in the centuries after bixel's death.

bixel's name persists in the language as the marker of its engineered core. <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a> speakers refer to the foundational vocabulary as the bixel 1000 the way english speakers might refer to a dictionary by its compiler. the choice was honorary; bixel did not name it. the lexicon she set down, the <a class="wiki-link" href="/language/mil-tok">mil-tok</a>, survives as the master record of the language she built.

the deeper philosophical commitment of the language, that <span class="proper">kahu</span> does not split into smaller and larger kahu but is one word at all scales, is attributed to bixel by tradition. whether bixel made the decision deliberately or it was already present in the speech communities that fed the language is open canon. either way, the language that survives is the one that holds the principle.
