bixel built the kahu-tok language. the work began in the years after <a class="wiki-link" href="/events/the-first-descent">the first descent</a>, when the survivors who had carried the bastard tongue down out of the bunkers needed a shared speech that could grow with the new community. bixel's partner, <a class="wiki-link" href="/people/akika">akika</a>, had been killed by the russian state before the fracture; the deep-grief vocabulary bixel built into kahu-tok derives from that loss.

bixel was a native russian speaker. by her own account in the <a class="wiki-link" href="/language/mil-tok">mil-tok</a>'s margins she built the language in her own tongue first, testing every root against the language she grieved in, then took most of her tongue back out. what stayed is the ice and winter and travel vocabulary, the lullaby word, the hard yes, and one vowel: the deep i of her mother tongue survives in a single word, <span class="kt"><a class="wiki-link" href="/language/vyl">vyl</a></span>. the russian edition of the mil-tok was the first she wrote.

the language bixel designed has two organizing principles. importance-first syntax places the most important element of a sentence at its head, so that the speaker's emphasis is visible from the first word. and the bixel 1000, the engineered core vocabulary of roughly three hundred fifty working roots, ninety of them kept from the old languages, with compounds and set phrases carrying the count to the thousand, gives kahu-tok its baseline. on top of the bixel 1000, organic accretions from the bunker populations the <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/zou-esh">zou-esh</a> brought home have entered the language by use rather than by design. the southern caravan's greeting, mi casa es su casa, was spoken whole across the nu-tera fences from the founding years; the centuries wore the phrase down to the single word <span class="kt"><a class="wiki-link" href="/language/micasu">micasu</a></span>, which solidified in the generations after bixel's death. she heard the phrase; the word outlived her. the language was carried to a finished and teachable form after bixel's death by her student <a class="wiki-link" href="/people/yelena-cruz">yelena cruz</a>, whom the kahu's later account of kahu-tok does not record. the kahu credit the language to bixel.

bixel's name persists in the language as the marker of its engineered core. <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a> speakers refer to the foundational vocabulary as the bixel 1000. the choice was honorary; bixel did not name it. the name bixel is also the first kahu mononym. bixel encouraged the shortening of a full spoken name into a single label, the practice spread and became the kahu naming custom, and the name bixel is itself a contraction of bekselev.

the deeper philosophical commitment of the language, that <span class="proper">kahu</span> does not split into smaller and larger kahu but is one word at all scales, is attributed to bixel by tradition. whether bixel made the decision deliberately or it was already present in the speech communities that fed the language is open canon. the language that survives holds the principle.
