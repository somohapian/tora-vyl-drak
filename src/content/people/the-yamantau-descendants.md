the yamantau descendants are the bunker descendants of the russian yamantau facility who crossed the bering ice bridge in a multi-generational migration and arrived at <span class="proper">tora vyl drak</span> during the late age of tapestry. they are the best-documented of the long-distance arrivals during the messenger missions era and one of several survivor populations whose absorption into the kahu shaped the early kahu civilization.

the yamantau facility was a deep mountain bunker in the southern urals, built during the pre-collapse period by the russian state for continuity-of-government purposes. like other continuity-of-government bunkers worldwide, yamantau was emp-hardened and provisioned for long-term sealed operation, but not engineered as a multi-generational ark. its operational decay across the dark century followed the pattern of all such bunkers: rations exhausted in years, mechanical components failing in decades, social structures decaying across generations.

what made the yamantau descendants distinct was that they did not die in place. word of the mountain that breathes, the geothermal plume rising from hart-ston that was visible from mountain peaks hundreds of miles away on cold days, reached the russian far east through the slow trade networks that survived the silence. in degraded folk reports, the place became <span class="proper">tura vyl drak</span>, "the last dragon's hope" or "the valley of the second sun," a mythical paradise with long-lived god-like inhabitants. driven by the legend, the surviving yamantau descendants undertook a multi-generational migration eastward across siberia and finally over the bering ice bridge.

the journey took generations. children were born on the road. elders died on the road. the population that arrived in the cascade arc was numerically smaller than the population that had set out and culturally different from it. the yamantau descendants who reached the gate were the survivors of a long passage that had selected for endurance, for community discipline, and for the particular blend of cultural inheritance that could be carried across continents on foot.

they were brought into <span class="proper">tora vyl drak</span> through the standard kahu integration process. quarantined at the gate, settled into nu-tera, eventually integrated into the broader caldera community. their absorption was complete in the kahu sense: they became kahu, indistinguishable in present-day life from those whose ancestors had descended directly from the cascade arc bunkers. but the cultural traces of their origin entered the kahu pattern. specific phrases, foods, practices, and lineage memories that trace to the russian original.

the yamantau migration is one of the central narratives the kahu tell themselves about what the messenger missions made possible. distant survivors did reach them. the legend did its work. the mountain that breathes was visible far enough that people who did not know where it was could still come find it.
