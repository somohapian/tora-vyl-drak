mara did not found the <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/zou-esh">zou-esh</a>. the order existed before her, going back at least to the messengers of the <a class="wiki-link" href="/eras/age-of-tapestry">age of tapestry</a> who spread the legend of the mountain that breathes. what mara did was become the most famous and most prolific zou-esh on record. the traditions she is sometimes said to have established are codifications and refinements rather than inventions.

mara was born around 3230. she was still in training, not yet a full zou-esh, when <a class="wiki-link" href="/people/edi">edi</a>, the head of the order, seized control of the sanctuary in the <a class="wiki-link" href="/events/the-seizure">seizure</a>, and even then she was known for being brutal and brash. she joined the resistance against him, not as its public figure, before the long field career for which she is best known.

mara lived well after <a class="wiki-link" href="/people/bixel">bixel</a>, in the <a class="wiki-link" href="/eras/age-of-bloom">age of bloom</a>, with no overlap between them. by mara's time <a class="wiki-link" href="/language/kahu-tok">kahu-tok</a> was finished and ordinary: she grew up a native speaker of the language bixel had built generations earlier, the way a person grows up speaking a tongue they never think to question. the word <span class="kt"><a class="wiki-link" href="/language/mara">mara</a></span> for a shield, taken from her name, entered the language later still, after her death.

she led the diplomatic mission that brought the cat-farmers and their cats into <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/tora-vyl-drak">tora vyl drak</a>. this is the founding moment of the partnership between the zou-esh and the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a> cat. the cat-farmers were quarantined at <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/the-gate">the gate</a> and settled into the agricultural belt of <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/nu-tera">nu-tera</a> at the standard pace, like any arrivals. the cats were absorbed into kahu life over generations. the human throughline of the partnership has been continuous since.

mara was small, about a hundred pounds, and her cat outweighed her. her configuration was two batons, the disabler's choice, and her fighting was percussive and continuous: chains of strikes too fast to track, heard as a rhythm of cracks rather than seen as blows. her signature sequence ran disorient, disable, reassemble, end: the batons returned to staff through her <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/the-trinity-lock">trinity lock</a>, and the staff ended it at the base of the skull, fast and painless. the lock no enemy could open was the motion with which she closed a fight.

her last mission was her seventy-eighth into the field. she was returning to the caldera with her zou-esh group during summer season when raiders caught them at the chokepoint where <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/the-long-climb">the long climb</a> meets the plateau. mara screamed <span class="kt">!kia-vyl</span>, "live," at her team as a command to continue without her, and she fought the raiders alone. she killed them. she was mortally wounded and did not move from that place. word traveled up the long climb. large numbers of kahu came down to see her. she died at the gate.

the inscription on the stone, <span class="kt">!kia-vyl</span>, is not commemoration. it is the last command she gave, scrawled into the rock at the place where she gave it. the modern zou-esh live and train at the gate. defending the gate is the defining role of the order.

she was on active duty a hundred and two years and ran seventy-eight missions, and she was controversial alive. her sparring broke bones; a student died at her hand in training, and her answer, better me than on the ground under a raider, divided the kahu while the order largely accepted it. her death at the gate made the brutality legible as preparation, and sainthood crystallized around a woman the living had argued about. anyone telling her story has to choose which mara to tell, and the archive declines to choose.

her career was run at a punishing pace, well beyond what the long kahu life was meant to sustain in the field. this is why she reached a seventy-eighth mission at all, and why she was killed in the lowlands. the order's modern lifetime cap of seventy-five missions is the safety limit set after her death, and she is the reason it exists. the cap of seventy-five is three short of her seventy-eight. no living zou-esh exceeds her total.
