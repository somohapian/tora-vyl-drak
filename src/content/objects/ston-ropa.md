<span class="kt">ston-ropa</span> is the shear-thickening cloth that forms the zou-esh's shirts and pants. the name in kahu-tok reads rock-clothes. the cloth is thin and looks like ordinary clothing. under slow pressure it yields; a finger can push into it as into any garment. under sudden impact it resists like solid rock.

the shear-thickening effect is produced by an algae-derived component woven into or saturating the textile. the algae component is part of the same biotech tradition that produces kahu medicine, the agent stack, ston-shen historically, and the broader living-materials economy of the warden-tenders. the precise algae species or strain is open canon. what is canonical is that the component is grown, not synthesized; living, not mineral; and that the cloth's behavior depends on the component's continuing biological functioning.

the operational effect is that ston-ropa functions as armor that is not visible as such. a slap, a punch, an arrow, a sudden strike with a club or a blade, all are met with stiffness approaching solid material at the moment of contact. the zou-esh feels the impact as a pressure rather than a penetration. the cloth dissipates the force across a wider area, then returns to its yielded state once the impact has passed.

ston-ropa fails against slow penetration. a knife pressed steadily, with controlled pressure rather than sudden impact, can work its way into the cloth. it also fails against prolonged pressure (sustained crushing rather than impact strike) and against specific weapons designed to exploit the shear-thickening threshold (open canon in particulars; zou-esh field protocols include awareness of these failure modes). the cloth protects against a specific range of threats rather than all of them.

the kahu's choice of ston-ropa over more visibly armored alternatives is partly tactical and partly philosophical. tactically, the cloth's appearance as ordinary clothing matters for the watch-first protocol: a zou-esh observing a target for days does not want to look like a soldier. philosophically, the cloth reflects the kahu's general approach to technology, in which protection is designed not to announce itself. on this principle, ston-ropa conceals its function in the same way the kahu cat operates without being the visible center of an operation.

whether <span class="kt">ston-ropa</span> is used outside zou-esh applications is open canon. the cat-kahu may use lighter versions for their own work with the cats. the broader kahu civilian population probably does not need it and probably does not have it; the living biotech component implies maintenance and probably cost that puts the cloth in working-equipment territory rather than everyday wear.
