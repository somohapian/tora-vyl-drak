living textiles are the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a>'s living garments, grown and shaped by the <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/the-kahu-silth">kahu-silth</a>. in a world without microchips, where biology does the work that machines once did, cloth is not inert. a living textile is a system. it monitors the body, protects it, heals it, and adapts to it over the life of the garment.

the materials are biological. human hair serves as the structural fiber, the thread the cloth is built on. algae are integrated for warmth, moisture handling, and pigment. bacteria are cultivated through the weave for self-cleaning and antimicrobial protection, so the garment keeps itself clean rather than being cleaned. viral vector systems give the cloth ecological stability and let it adapt to the specific wearer across time. all of it comes from the <span class="kt">vyl-tapi</span> biotech tradition, the same lineage of living technology that produces kahu medicine and food.

the kahu-silth produce several categories of garment. the bio-veil is protective, a worn barrier against the world. the healing weave is medical, a garment that tends a wound or a body over time. the thermic sheath manages heat and cold against the skin. and the braidcloth is ceremonial, woven in part from the hair of ancestors, so that a person wearing braidcloth wears their lineage literally against their body. the braidcloth is the clearest expression of what living textiles mean to the kahu: cloth is not separate from the people. it is grown from them, and in the braidcloth, from the ones who came before.

the <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/zou-esh">zou-esh</a> kit belongs to the same craft. the shear-thickening <span class="kt"><a class="wiki-link" href="/objects/ston-ropa">ston-ropa</a></span> that looks like ordinary clothing and hardens to rock under impact, and the <span class="kt"><a class="wiki-link" href="/objects/mirar-sin-ropa">mirar-sin-ropa</a></span> cloak that lets a zou-esh disappear into terrain, are living textiles built for the field. whether the kahu-silth make the zou-esh kit directly, or whether the order trains its own in the craft, is open canon.

the kahu-tok names for the four categories, the full range of what the kahu-silth produce, and the lifespan and renewal of a living garment are open canon.
