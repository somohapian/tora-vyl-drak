<span class="kt">ston-shen</span> is the chemosynthetic lichen-bacteria paste that sustained the <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/kahu">kahu</a>'s ancestors through <a class="wiki-link" href="/eras/the-dark-century">the dark century</a>. the name in kahu-tok reads rock-life. it is the food that kept humanity alive during the long underground, between the failure of the bunker rations and <a class="wiki-link" href="/events/the-first-descent">the first descent</a> into the caldera.

ston-shen is biologically novel. it is a symbiotic relationship between a fungus and a chemoautotrophic bacterium that bypasses photosynthesis entirely. unlike plants, which need sunlight, and unlike most fungi, which need organic matter to decompose, the ston-shen system derives cellular energy through chemosynthesis from the sulfur and mineral compounds present in boiling geothermal vents. light is not required. organic input is not required. the inputs are the vent's mineral chemistry and the heat that drives the reactions.

what this meant for the bunker survivors was that ston-shen could be cultivated wherever geothermal vents could be reached. the bunker locations of <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/the-cascade">the cascade</a> arc were chosen, in part, for proximity to such vents. the survival logic of the dark century ran through ston-shen because the rations would run out, the fungiculture beds and underground livestock could supplement but not sustain, and only ston-shen offered a food source with no upstream dependency on the surface world.

ston-shen tasted strongly of sulfur. the bunker survivors did not love it. they ate it. the kahu carry the taste forward as a cultural memory; the present-day kahu, who eat the agricultural abundance of <a class="wiki-link" href="/places/sun-tera">sun-tera</a>, do not eat ston-shen as a staple. whether it is still produced ceremonially or as a historical reference is open canon.

the cultivation of ston-shen is part of the kahu's deeper technological inheritance. the same biological tradition that produced the agent stack, that produced <a class="wiki-link" href="/concepts/warden">warden</a>, that produced the algae-derived components of the <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/zou-esh">zou-esh</a> kit, traces back through the bunker generations to the moment ston-shen made survival possible. the kahu's biotech is not a modern invention. it is the long evolution of a tradition that began with a sulfur-tasting paste in a dark place.
