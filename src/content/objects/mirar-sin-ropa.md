<span class="kt">mirar-sin-ropa</span> is the passive camouflage cloak of the zou-esh. the name in kahu-tok reads see-without-clothes or no-see-clothes, indicating the function. the cloak makes a stationary zou-esh extremely difficult to distinguish from the terrain they are sitting in.

the cloak is part of the zou-esh's standard kit and is worn over the layered <span class="kt">ston-ropa</span> shirt and pants. it is fitted to drape so that a zou-esh at rest, sitting against rock or crouched in foliage or pressed against snow, presents a silhouette and surface that the cloak adapts to match. the precise mechanism by which the matching happens is open canon. the practical result is that an observer scanning a landscape will fail to notice a zou-esh on watch even at relatively close distances. the cloak is the technological foundation of the order's watch-first protocol.

the cloak's effect is most reliable when the zou-esh is still. movement degrades the camouflage; a zou-esh moving across open ground is visible at human-eye distances even in the cloak. but zou-esh fieldwork emphasizes patience over motion. the cloak is worn for the long days of observation a zou-esh spends watching a potential target before initiating contact. it is also worn during approach when stealth is required, with the zou-esh moving in slow controlled increments that the cloak can keep up with.

the cloak is effective against unaugmented visual observation. whether it is effective against pre-collapse-era electronic sensors is open canon and largely academic: the kahu's missions rarely encounter active sensor systems below the spore line. the lowlands the zou-esh operates in have lost the technological infrastructure that would maintain such systems. the cloak is designed for the human eye, which is the threat that almost always actually matters.

the cloak is grown or manufactured through the same biotech tradition that produces <span class="kt">ston-ropa</span> and the agent stack. the specifics of its production are open canon. the warden-tenders are presumably involved at some level in maintaining the strains and processes that produce it.
