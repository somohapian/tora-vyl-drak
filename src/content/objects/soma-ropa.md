<span class="kt">soma-ropa</span> is the layered suit of the zou-esh taken as a whole. the name in kahu-tok reads body-clothes. it is the umbrella term that covers the three principal garment layers: the <span class="kt">klin-soma</span> bio-monitoring undersuit worn against the skin, the <span class="kt">ston-ropa</span> shear-thickening armor cloth that forms the outer shirts and pants, and the <span class="kt">mirar-sin-ropa</span> passive camouflage cloak worn over both.

the suit functions as an integrated system. the undersuit monitors. the armor cloth protects. the cloak hides. each layer is necessary; the absence of any one substantially reduces the zou-esh's operational capacity. the design intent is that a zou-esh in full <span class="kt">soma-ropa</span> can sit in observation for days, move quickly when required, sustain impacts during contact, and remain in continuous biotech support throughout. no single garment provides all of these functions; the layered system does.

the layers are donned in a specific order. <span class="kt">klin-soma</span> first, against the skin, where its sensors can establish contact. <span class="kt">ston-ropa</span> next, the working layer that the zou-esh moves and fights in. <span class="kt">mirar-sin-ropa</span> last, worn over both and adjustable for the conditions of the current operation. whether there are formal protocols around the order of donning, beyond the practical requirement that the layers go on in the order that allows the suit to function, is open canon.

the suit's components age at different rates. <span class="kt">klin-soma</span> is replaced more frequently than the outer layers because skin contact and continuous use wear it faster. <span class="kt">ston-ropa</span> wears more slowly but takes the impact damage of active combat and is repaired or replaced after significant hits. <span class="kt">mirar-sin-ropa</span> is the most durable layer in mechanical terms but requires the most careful biotech maintenance to preserve its camouflage effect. across a seventy-five-mission career, a zou-esh probably goes through many <span class="kt">klin-soma</span>, several <span class="kt">ston-ropa</span> garments, and a small number of <span class="kt">mirar-sin-ropa</span> cloaks. the suit at the end of the career is not, materially, the same suit as at graduation, though it is treated as continuous with the zou-esh who wears it.
