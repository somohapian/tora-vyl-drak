<span class="kt">klin-soma</span> is the bio-monitoring undersuit worn against the skin under the zou-esh's other layers. the name in kahu-tok reads clean-body or clean-living-body, indicating the suit's continuous diagnostic function. it tracks the zou-esh's body state across the entire duration of operations.

the suit is form-fitting and made from the same biotech tradition as <span class="kt">ston-ropa</span> and <span class="kt">mirar-sin-ropa</span>. the specific sensors built into the fabric are open canon. plausibly the suit tracks heart rate, respiration, body temperature, hydration, blood chemistry inferred from skin contact, exposure to environmental toxins including <span class="kt">peli-shen</span> concentrations, and the stress and exertion patterns that the warden-tenders flag as signs a zou-esh may be operating outside safe parameters. some or all of this is captured continuously across the days or weeks of a deep-field mission.

how the suit displays its readings is open canon. the kahu have no pre-collapse-style electronic displays available. plausible mechanisms include biotech color shifts visible at specific points on the suit, sensations the suit transmits through pressure or temperature to the wearer's skin, or stored data that the warden-tenders read out from the suit after the zou-esh returns to the gate. some combination of these is likely; the specifics are open canon.

the suit's protective role is secondary to its monitoring role, but it is present. it provides thermal regulation, moisture management, and the baseline skin-contact comfort that allows the zou-esh to wear the layered kit for extended periods without irritation. these functions are necessary, not glamorous, and are mostly invisible until they are missing.

klin-soma is the most personal piece of the zou-esh's kit. it sits closest to the body, captures the most intimate data about the wearer's physiological state, and travels with the zou-esh on every mission. retired zou-esh in their post-active careers often note that returning the klin-soma at retirement is one of the harder moments of the transition; the suit has carried them through their entire active service.
