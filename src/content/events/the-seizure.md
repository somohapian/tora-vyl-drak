the seizure is the kahu name for the period in the middle of the <a class="wiki-link" href="/eras/age-of-bloom">age of bloom</a>, around 3250, when <a class="wiki-link" href="/people/edi">edi</a>, the head of the <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/zou-esh">zou-esh</a>, took control of the caldera and the plateau and ended the kahu's first government, together with the resistance that undid him.

the first government had run for roughly eleven hundred years from the founding: a <a class="wiki-link" href="/organizations/the-council">council</a> of the heads of the founding houses, presided over by a single leader chosen from among them, expanded over the centuries, including a seat added for the zou-esh. by the late bloom it was chronically deadlocked along the old division between the caldera floor and the plateau.

the capture was administrative, not military. the head of the order controlled the body that carried information from the outside world to the council and held the gate, and used the floor-plateau deadlock to accumulate temporary authorities that, once granted, were never returned. it was largely bloodless. most kahu did not register it, because daily life did not change. resistance came from the political class and from within the zou-esh itself, which was not united behind its head. the young zou-esh <a class="wiki-link" href="/people/mara">mara</a>, not yet through her training, was among the resistance, though not its public figure.

the resistance prevailed. the head was deposed and sent into exile with the means to survive, the kahu having no death penalty. the episode is the origin of the kahu's second government, the bicameral council of the present, structured so that no single figure or order could capture the sanctuary again. it is also recorded as the one time the zou-esh, the order tasked with guarding the kahu's survival, produced the most serious threat to their self-government from within.
